**Problem carried forward from D1a-2:** Beginner-to-intermediate half-marathon/
marathon runners without a coach struggle to find training plans that adapt to
their individual body and progress — generic or overly aggressive plans push
volume/intensity that risks injury rather than adjusting to the runner.

## 1. Jobs To Be Done

The primary job is: **finish my current training cycle and race healthy,
without getting sidelined by injury** — for a runner who has already completed
one or two marathons on a basic, static plan and now wants something that
actually adjusts to them.

Functionally, today's basic plans don't account for how the runner is feeling
day to day, or respond when early signs of injury show up. The runner doesn't
lack motivation to adjust — they lack the knowledge to know how to adjust
safely and still reach race day prepared. The job isn't "follow a plan," it's
"know when and how to deviate from the plan without losing the race."

Emotionally, this job is tied to confidence and fear reduction: going into the
race with confidence that training was done right, and training itself
without the constant background fear of an injury building up unnoticed.
